# Cross-Domain Onboarding: Glossaries, Taxonomies, Ontologies, Concept Maps and AI Domain Bridging

Scope note: the research was done on 2026-11-13 with a limited budget of about 17 search and fetch calls. Statements marked **(evidence)** rest on meta-analyses, benchmarks or controlled studies. Statements marked **(claim)** are author or vendor assertions, design proposals or small pilots. Where only a secondary summary was available, that is stated.

## 1. Learning-science evidence: do concept maps, knowledge maps and advance organizers speed up understanding?

### Takeaway
There is strong meta-analytic evidence that node-link concept and knowledge maps improve learning moderately compared with text, lists and outlines. Building a map helps more than studying a ready-made one, but studying a ready-made map still helps. The gains are larger for learners with low prior knowledge or low verbal ability, which describes the "engineer entering law" case. Map structure matters: hierarchical maps impose less mental effort than network-shaped maps, which argues against showing a raw, densely connected ontology graph to novices.

### Cited Findings
- **(evidence)** Nesbit & Adesope (2006), *Review of Educational Research* 76:413–448. The meta-analysis covers 55 studies, 67 effect sizes and 5,818 participants, from Grade 4 to postsecondary, in science, psychology, statistics and nursing. Concept-map use was associated with better knowledge retention across several conditions. Effects ranged from small to large depending on how maps were used and what they were compared with, and heterogeneity was significant in most subsets. The abstract gives no single pooled effect. — [Nesbit & Adesope 2006 abstract page](https://www.sfu.ca/~jcnesbit/articles/nesbitadesope2006.htm)
- **(evidence, secondary summary; not verified against the primary tables)** Later reviews describe Nesbit & Adesope as finding roughly g ≈ 0.82 for constructing maps and g ≈ 0.37 (about d ≈ 0.4) for studying maps made by an instructor or researcher, compared with text. They also report that studying maps benefits learners with lower domain knowledge or lower verbal ability more. — reported in secondary sources found via search, e.g. [Schroeder et al. 2018 PDF mirror](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10648-017-9403-9.pdf). The report writer should verify these numbers before quoting them as exact.
- **(evidence)** Schroeder, Nesbit, Anguiano & Adesope (2018), "Studying and Constructing Concept Maps: a Meta-Analysis," *Educational Psychology Review* 30(2):431–455 (published online 2017). It pools 142 independent effect sizes (n = 11,814) and finds an overall **g = 0.58** (p < .001). **Creating maps gives g = 0.72 and studying maps gives g = 0.43** relative to comparison conditions. — [SFU Summit record](https://summit.sfu.ca/item/17789); [TUM Clearinghouse summary](https://www.clearinghouse.edu.tum.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/CHU_KR-19_ENG_Schroeder-2_2017.pdf)
- **(evidence)** Amadieu et al. (2009), *Learning and Instruction*, compared hierarchical with network-structured concept maps for learners with low and high prior knowledge. **Both groups invested less mental effort with the hierarchical map.** Prior knowledge moderated disorientation and learning. — [Amadieu et al. 2009 PDF](https://research.ou.nl/ws/files/1022307/Amadieu-etal_LI_2009.pdf); [OU record](https://research.ou.nl/en/publications/effects-of-prior-knowledge-and-concept-map-structure-on-disorient/)
- **(evidence)** Expertise reversal: worked-out concept maps that are highly effective for inexperienced learners can lose their effectiveness for more experienced learners. This is a form of the redundancy effect. — [Hilbert & Renkl 2009](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2009-hilbert.pdf)
- **(evidence)** A 2025 study with 136 university students used **concept maps plus a glossary as "pre-training"** before the main material. Pre-training reduced extraneous cognitive load for all learners. Lower-prior-knowledge learners benefited most, and the expected expertise reversal for higher-prior-knowledge learners did not clearly appear. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2025, "Rethinking pre-training"](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1628047/full); [PMC copy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12367772/)
- **(evidence, secondhand)** Advance organizers: Luiten, Ames & Ackerman (1980, *AERJ* 17:211–218) and Stone (1983) both concluded that advance organizers facilitate learning and retention at all ability levels, with no special benefit for low-ability learners. The source spells the third author "Ackerman", not "Ackerson". — [Chen & Hirumi, ITDL 2009](https://itdl.org/Journal/Jun_09/article01.htm)

### Inferences
- A **glossary plus a small taxonomy or concept map shown before the detailed answer** acts as the "pre-training" or "advance organizer" studied above. It is the best-supported way to speed up general understanding for domain newcomers. The expected benefit is moderate (about g 0.4–0.6), not dramatic.
- Making users **build or edit** part of the map, for example "drag the roles you think are involved in a trial" or "confirm or correct the assistant's map", should beat passive viewing, since constructing beat studying in both meta-analyses. This is an extrapolation from classroom settings.
- **Hierarchical views (taxonomy trees) should come first**. A free-form ontology graph should be an opt-in view, because network maps cost more effort even for knowledgeable users.
- Experts in their home domain who are novices in the target domain (the overlapping-domain case) are still novices on target concepts. Scaffolding should therefore be set per concept, not per user.

### Gaps
- There is no exact pooled effect size for Luiten et al. (1980). The primary article was not retrieved.
- No controlled study was found on **graph size or density** (for example 20 versus 200 nodes) and comprehension. Cognitive-load concerns about large graphs are inferred from the hierarchical-versus-network results and from expertise-reversal theory, not measured directly.
- Nesbit & Adesope effect sizes broken down by comparison type (text versus outline versus list) and by outcome (retention versus transfer) were not verified. The abstract page did not list them.
- Worked-example meta-analyses were not searched within the budget.

## 2. Interdisciplinary communication: boundary objects, trading zones and shared vocabularies

### Takeaway
The theory is well established. Boundary objects (Star & Griesemer 1989) and trading zones (Galison 1997) explain how groups coordinate **without full consensus**, using artifacts that are flexible locally but recognizable across communities. Bio-ontologies such as the Gene Ontology were founded explicitly as shared languages across communities. Within the budget I found **mostly descriptive or case-based support** that shared ontologies improve interdisciplinary teamwork, and no controlled outcome studies.

### Cited Findings
- **(theory)** Boundary objects were introduced by Star & Griesemer (1989) in a study of Berkeley's Museum of Vertebrate Zoology. They permit coordination without consensus and are both concrete and abstract, fluid and well-defined. The authors identify four types: repositories, ideal types, coincident boundaries and standardized forms. Boundary objects are not politically neutral or necessarily consensual. — [Wikipedia: Boundary object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_object)
- **(theory)** Galison coined "trading zones" in *Image and Logic* (1997). They are spaces where groups with different practices exchange without full agreement, and he later explicitly linked them to boundary objects. — [Wikipedia: Boundary object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_object); [PhilSci trading zones paper](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/22506/1/230905%20Trading%20zones%20ver%204.1%20final%20de-anonymised.pdf)
- **(case/claim)** An STS study frames health-care IT design as a trading zone between software design and sociology in which neither discipline serves the other. — [Social Studies of Science 2010](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0162243910377624). Another paper argues that interdisciplinary research operates through boundary objects that make epistemic negotiation possible. — [SAGE 2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0162243920941581)
- **(case)** The Gene Ontology began when model-organism database groups agreed to "connect our data by sharing the same language for describing it." They started from shared semantic concepts that defined the words needed to describe particular domains of biology. — [GO Consortium, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC311077); [PMC549054](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC549054)
- **(analysis/claim)** Bio-ontology consortia are described as organizations that maintain labelling systems for moving data across research contexts. Model organisms are described as boundary objects through which biological disciplines meet. — [Leonelli, CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/12825856.pdf)
- **(evidence on risk)** In a qualitative study with 14 practitioners using LLM-assisted knowledge-graph tools (LinkQ), users, including KG experts, often **overtrusted LLM output because the visualizations looked helpful**, even when the answers were wrong. — [arXiv 2505.21512](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.21512)

### Inferences
- In the assistant, the glossary or ontology should be treated as a **boundary object**: one shared concept ID with **perspective-specific labels and definitions**, such as "sensitivity (clinical) ≈ recall (ML)". This matches how UMLS keeps each source's names (section 3). The goal is coordination across groups, not forcing one vocabulary on everyone.
- Visual polish raises trust. Graph views should show provenance (source manual or page) and mark mappings the LLM inferred as unverified.

### Gaps
- No controlled or quantitative study was found showing that shared ontologies measurably improve interdisciplinary team performance or speed of understanding. The available support is historical or case-based.
- Studies of glossaries in clinician–engineer teams (for example in medical-device or health-IT projects) were not found within the budget.

## 3. Ontology alignment between domains and LLM-based matching; background-adapted and analogy explanations

### Takeaway
Biomedical terminologies are already heavily cross-linked through UMLS (about 3.5M concepts from 190 sources), and FHIR ConceptMap provides a standard mechanism for directional mappings. LLM-based ontology matching became competitive in 2023–2025, but **its accuracy varies widely by task**. Zero-shot LLMs4OM reached F1 about 89 on Anatomy but only about 26 on SNOMED–FMA. The best 2025 systems use LLMs as an **oracle for uncertain candidates** inside classical matchers such as LogMap. Hallucinated mappings are a documented failure mode. Cross-*discipline* mapping (medicine to CS) has no benchmark. Expertise-adapted LLM explanations show positive but small-sample user-study results. Evidence that LLM-generated **analogies** help *human* learners is thin.

### Cited Findings
**Biomedical terminology infrastructure**
- **(fact)** The UMLS Metathesaurus 2025AB holds about 3.49M concepts and 17.39M unique names from 190 source vocabularies (2025AA: about 3.45M concepts). Linked sources include SNOMED CT, ICD-10-CM, LOINC, MeSH, RxNorm and CPT. UMLS keeps each source's names and relationships rather than replacing them. The full release needs a UMLS license, and source restriction levels apply. — [NLM UMLS statistics 2025AB](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/research/umls/knowledge_sources/metathesaurus/release/statistics.html); [NLM 2025AB release note](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/pubs/techbull/nd25/nd25_umls_release_ab.html); [CASRAI UMLS guide](https://casrai.org/guides/umls). One secondary source reports 3,550,208 concepts for a 2026AB release, which I could not verify.
- **(fact)** A FHIR Terminology Service must support CodeSystem `$lookup`, `$validate-code` and `$subsumes`, and ValueSet `$expand`. ConceptMap `$translate` evaluates directional mappings between code systems. Large terminologies (SNOMED CT, LOINC, RxNorm) are exposed through server operations rather than full CodeSystem resources. — [HL7 FHIR terminology service](https://www.hl7.org/fhir/terminology-service.html); [build.fhir.org](https://build.fhir.org/terminology-service.html)

**LLM ontology matching accuracy**
- **(evidence)** LLMs4OM (Babaei Giglou et al., ESWC 2024) tested zero-shot retrieval plus LLM matching on 20 datasets. Best F1 against the OAEI 2023 top system: Anatomy 89.11 vs 94.10; NCIT–DOID 83.01 vs 90.80; OMIM–ORDO 64.14 vs 71.50; **SNOMED–FMA 25.64 vs 78.50**; SNOMED–NCIT (neoplasm) 49.47 vs 77.10; SNOMED–NCIT (pharm) 68.02 vs 75.20. It beat the OAEI top system on Phenotype DOID–ORDO (89.83 vs 75.50) and HP–MP (85.01 vs 81.80). GPT-3.5 was best on most tasks and Mistral-7B on Phenotype. The domain-specific BioMistral did not consistently help. — [arXiv 2404.10317](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10317v1)
- **(claim)** OLaLa (Hertling & Paulheim 2023) reports zero- and few-shot prompting results comparable to supervised systems on OAEI tasks when few-shot prompting is used. — [OLaLa summary](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2311.03837v1)
- **(evidence)** In OAEI 2025, LogMap extended with an LLM oracle, which sends only uncertain mappings to an LLM, **ranked in the top 2 overall in the Bio-ML track**. It was tested with 5 LLMs and 6 prompt templates. The authors note that unlimited LLM use is too costly. — [arXiv 2508.08500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08500v2)
- **(claim)** GenOM (Manchester, v2 January 2026) uses an LLM to generate concept definitions, retrieves candidates by embedding and has an LLM judge each candidate. The authors report competitive Bio-ML results, often better than traditional and earlier LLM methods. These results were not cross-checked against the official OAEI tables. — [arXiv 2508.10703](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10703v2)
- **(evidence)** The OAEI-LLM benchmark (2024–2025) catalogues LLM hallucinations in ontology matching as missing, spurious or incorrect mappings. The TBox variant covers 10 LLMs. It is limited to 1:1 equivalence, and the authors expect subsumption or complex matches to cause more hallucination. They suggest that more ontology-structure context may reduce errors. — [arXiv 2409.14038](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.14038); [arXiv 2503.21813](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2503.21813v3)
- **(claim)** LLMs generate good candidate mappings mainly for **lexically similar** entities, and semantic discrimination remains a limitation. — [ELMKE workshop paper, CEUR Vol-4246](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4246/elmke-7.pdf)

**Expertise-adapted and analogy-based explanations**
- **(evidence, small)** Self-adaptive LLM task-plan explanations: participants' understanding increased after explanations were adapted to their expertise. Classifying explanations to the intended audience was about 50% correct (construction) and about 62% (agriculture), with non-experts hardest to match. — [arXiv 2604.21092](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.21092)
- **(evidence, small)** Personalizing robot explanations for expert versus non-expert audiences through parameter-efficient fine-tuning improved suitability in a user study with 17 roboticists. — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_1e0e80b3-0e7b-4d81-bc1b-befc505f7c67?preview=1)
- **(claim + user study)** The "Mind the XAI Gap" framework (2025) adapts explanations for experts and non-experts by cognitive load and expertise across healthcare, finance and software. It reports a user study with 56 participants. — [arXiv 2506.12240](https://www.emergentmind.com/articles/2506.12240)
- **(claim)** ExPerT (2026 preprint) infers a user's domain expertise from query wording and keystroke behavior and restructures answers for novices. — [arXiv 2607.01242](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01242)
- **(evidence, model-only)** In Yuan et al. (EMNLP 2024), GPT-4-generated analogies improved weaker LLMs' science QA. For example, GPT-3.5 on ARC rose from 83.33% to 85.56%, and free-form analogies were most effective. **The learners were models, not humans.** — [arXiv 2406.11375](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11375v1)
- **(study, results not retrieved)** Cao et al. (2023) used LLM-generated analogies turned into visual metaphors for STEM learners and evaluated them with a randomized A/B/C test of learning gains and motivation. Outcomes were not retrieved. — [arXiv 2308.10454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10454v1)

### Inferences
- For medicine and CS, the **medical side should be anchored in UMLS/SNOMED/MeSH IDs**, and the CS side in manual-derived terms, with optional links to Wikidata or ACM CCS (not researched here). Cross-domain links such as "sensitivity ↔ recall", "differential diagnosis ↔ classification/ranking" and "triage ↔ prioritization queue" should be stored as **typed, directional, confidence-scored mappings** (SKOS exactMatch/closeMatch/relatedMatch semantics, or a FHIR-ConceptMap-like structure). Many of them are analogies, not equivalences.
- The OAEI results imply that **LLM-proposed cross-domain mappings need human review or retrieval grounding**. The proven pattern is a classical matcher plus an LLM oracle on uncertain pairs. Cross-*discipline* analogies are harder than intra-biomedical matching, and no benchmark exists for them.
- "Explain X in terms of my domain" is plausible and supported by small adaptation studies. Learning gains from LLM analogies for humans remain **unproven**. The assistant should label analogies as analogies and state where they break down.

### Gaps
- There is no benchmark or study of LLM alignment **between a medical and a computer-science ontology**.
- Official OAEI 2024/2025 Bio-ML leaderboard F1 values were not retrieved, and no MeSH-specific matching results were found.
- No controlled study was found measuring human comprehension from LLM analogies drawn from the learner's home domain.
- Cao et al. 2023 outcomes were not retrieved.

## 4. Legal ontologies and taxonomies usable for onboarding, and jurisdiction differences

### Takeaway
Several reusable assets exist: LKIF Core for basic legal concepts (roles, actions, norms), FOLIO and SALI LMSS taxonomies with about 18k concepts each, the LegalRuleML OASIS standard for rules, and ECLI for case identifiers. None is designed as a pedagogical "court trial" map. Roles and trial processes **differ substantially between common-law and civil-law systems**, so an onboarding taxonomy must be jurisdiction-parameterized.

### Cited Findings
- **(fact)** LKIF Core (Hoekstra, Breuker, Di Bello, Boer; ESTRELLA project; ICAIL 2007; IOS Press 2009) has 15 modules. Basic clusters cover process, role, action and expression, extended into legal action (public acts, public bodies, legal and natural persons), legal role (including legal professions) and norm. It is available as OWL on GitHub. — [GitHub lkif-core](https://github.com/RinkeHoekstra/lkif-core); [W3C list announcement 2007](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/semantic-web/2007Feb/0012.html); [VU research record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/lkif-core-principled-ontology-development-for-the-legal-domain/)
- **(fact)** FOLIO (Federated Open Legal Information Ontology, ALEA Institute) is CC-BY OWL with more than 18,000 concepts, each with an IRI, and opens in Protégé or WebProtégé. Stated use cases include generative AI, legal research and case management. — [GitHub alea-institute/folio](https://github.com/alea-institute/folio)
- **(fact)** SALI LMSS (Legal Matter Standard Specification) is a taxonomy for labeling legal work. Its facets include services, areas of law, parties (roles and industries) and location/jurisdiction. v1 was released 2020-02-04, v2 2022-03-09 with IRIs, and the API standard 2024-05-10. It has more than 18,000 tags and a CC BY-ND license. — [SALI about](https://sali.org/about/); [LMSS repo](https://github.imc.re/sali-legal/LMSS). Whether FOLIO and LMSS are formally mapped was not established. Both cite about 18k concepts, which suggests shared lineage, but this is unverified.
- **(fact)** LegalRuleML Core V1.0 became an OASIS Standard, announced 28 September 2021. It is built on Consumer RuleML 1.02 with XML Schema, RelaxNG and an RDFS metamodel, and is often paired with Akoma Ntoso and LKIF Core. — [OASIS announcement](https://www.oasis-open.org/?p=7649); [OASIS spec published](https://www.oasis-open.org/?p=7617); [RAWE paper, CEUR](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-1004/paper4.pdf)
- **(fact)** ECLI has the format `ECLI:[country]:[court]:[year]:[id]`, for example ECLI:EU:C:2005:446. The e-Justice ECLI search engine launched 4 May 2016, and a resolver at e-justice.europa.eu/ecli/ is available. — [European e-Justice ECLI page](https://e-justice.europa.eu/topics/legislation-and-case-law/european-case-law-identifier-ecli/eu_en); [Wikipedia ECLI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Case_Law_Identifier)
- **(fact)** Jurisdiction differences: civil-law judges act as fact-finders who supervise evidence and question witnesses. Civil-law systems often split judges into investigating judges (magistrates) and trial judges, focus on pre-trial investigation and treat prosecutors as less adversarial, sometimes as advisors to the court. Common-law trials rely on adversarial advocates before an impartial judge or jury. — [US DOJ: Roles in Civil Legal Systems](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-ovt/roles-civil-legal-systems); [Federal Judicial Center: civil/common law](https://judiciariesworldwide.fjc.gov/civil-common-law)

### Inferences
- For a "lawyer/judge role taxonomy", the role classes can be seeded from LKIF Core legal-role and legal-person classes and the FOLIO/LMSS party-role facets. Jurisdiction-specific subclasses, such as investigating judge (civil law) and jury (common law), would then be added. The trial "ontology graph" (phases such as filing, pre-trial, hearing, judgment and appeal, linked to actors) would need custom modelling. LKIF provides the process/action/role primitives, not a ready-made trial workflow.
- The onboarding UI should start by asking for or inferring the **jurisdiction and legal tradition** and should flag concepts that do not transfer, for example "discovery" (US) versus a dossier-based investigation.

### Gaps
- No dedicated, maintained **court-trial process ontology** was found within the budget, and JURIX/ICAIL proceedings were not searched in depth.
- No onboarding or learning study using legal ontologies with non-lawyers was found.
- Current maintenance status of LKIF Core: the sources found date from 2007–2009.

## 5. Medical-AI crossover resources and design guidance (user-profile-aware answers, progressive disclosure, concept tours, prerequisite graphs)

### Takeaway
Clinical crossover infrastructure is mature (UMLS, SNOMED CT, LOINC, FHIR terminology services; see section 3). The design-side evidence for LLM tutors with prerequisite graphs or KG visualizations is **pilot-level or qualitative**. The strongest design rationale comes from the learning-science findings: pre-training with a glossary or map, hierarchical-first views, scaffolding that fades with expertise, and constructive interaction.

### Cited Findings
- **(pilot, n = 6)** AI-sensei uses a knowledge graph of Algebra 2 units with prerequisite edges to tailor ChatGPT-4 feedback. Students gave favorable ease-of-use, correctness and usefulness ratings, but personalization quality varied with question difficulty and student profile. — [arXiv 2412.03856](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.03856)
- **(qualitative)** Users preferred a chat widget embedded in their visualization workflow that lets them steer the LLM through graph interactions. — [arXiv 2404.01425](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.01425)
- **(qualitative)** Overtrust risk from KG visualizations paired with an LLM (LinkQ, n = 14). — [arXiv 2505.21512](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.21512)
- **(claim)** Fine-tuned LLMs perform well at prerequisite-relation detection for educational knowledge graphs (dissertation; no learner outcome study confirmed). — [Sabancı University repository](https://research.sabanciuniv.edu/id/eprint/51711/1/10656599.pdf)
- **(evidence)** Supporting learning-science design levers: concept map plus glossary pre-training reduces extraneous load ([Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1628047/full)). Hierarchical maps cost less effort than network maps ([Amadieu et al. 2009](https://research.ou.nl/ws/files/1022307/Amadieu-etal_LI_2009.pdf)). Constructing beats studying maps (g 0.72 vs 0.43) ([Schroeder et al. 2018](https://summit.sfu.ca/item/17789)). Expertise reversal ([Hilbert & Renkl 2009](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2009-hilbert.pdf)).

### Inferences (design recommendations, not tested in this product)
1. **User profile**: store the home domain, the target domain, the jurisdiction (for legal) and **per-concept familiarity**. Expertise reversal argues for fading scaffolds on concepts the user already knows.
2. **Answer pattern for cross-domain questions**: (a) a one-line definition in target-domain terms, (b) a "bridge" sentence mapping to the nearest home-domain concept, labelled exact, close or analogy, with where it breaks down, (c) a small local subgraph of 5–9 nodes around the concept, (d) links to source manual passages.
3. **Progressive disclosure**: show the taxonomy tree first, expand to a 1-hop ontology neighbourhood on click, and offer the full graph only on request. This is supported by the hierarchical-versus-network effort findings.
4. **Concept tour or onboarding path**: order concepts by a **prerequisite graph**, which LLMs can propose and a human should validate. Open with a glossary and overview map as an advance organizer. For the legal case, use roles, then trial phases, then documents and decisions.
5. **Active construction**: short "predict the relation" or "place this role" tasks, or letting users annotate the map, since constructing maps gave larger gains than studying them.
6. **Trust safeguards**: show provenance and the mapping confidence, and visually separate LLM-inferred cross-domain links from curated ones, given the overtrust and hallucination findings.

### Gaps
- No controlled study was found comparing an LLM assistant **with versus without** a KG or prerequisite visualization on comprehension speed for adult domain newcomers.
- CDS-specific ontologies (for example CDS Hooks, openEHR archetypes, clinical-guideline ontologies) were not researched within the budget.
- No evidence was found on the optimal node count for progressive-disclosure views. The 5–9 node suggestion is a heuristic, not a finding.
