# Turn the platform catalog into governed meaning

The approach works for internal platform domains, and it fits them better than it fits customer manuals. A large enterprise already holds most of the structure in machine-readable form: the developer-portal catalog, CRDs, Crossplane compositions, Terraform provider schemas and OpenAPI specs. The glossary, taxonomy, ontology and knowledge graph can therefore be *harvested and curated* rather than extracted from prose. What is missing, and what this design adds, is the semantic layer no portal provides. It covers canonical terms and synonyms, a class hierarchy of resource kinds, compound-resource structure, a capability-versus-service distinction that exposes overlap, provenance, and the links from documentation to all of these. The evidence is uneven. Self-reported internal assistants show real support relief: Uber Genie reached 48.9% helpfulness on about 45K monthly questions ([Uber](https://www.uber.com/blog/genie-ubers-gen-ai-on-call-copilot)), GoDaddy's handles over 80% of platform requests autonomously ([GoDaddy](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/news/how-we-scaled-platform-support-10x-without-scaling-the-team)), and Spotify reports 47% faster internal support resolution ([Spotify AiKA](https://backstage.spotify.com/aika-hub/)). However, no independent study measures consultation reduction, and Elastic's sober **7% drop in assisted case volume** is a better planning anchor than vendor claims of 70–95% ([Elastic](https://www.elastic.co/resources/article/elastic-support-assistant)). The standards (SKOS, OWL, SHACL, ORG, DCAT 3, PROV-O) and the git-based toolchain (LinkML, ROBOT, pySHACL, voc4cat, Vale) are mature. The platform-specific glue is not: no Backstage-to-RDF mapping, TOSCA OWL ontology or CRD-to-OWL converter exists, so expect to write small extractors. The decisive factors are organisational: a federated "semantic council" that does not become a bottleneck, named owners for cross-platform journeys, and a Reviewer AI that keeps humans deciding without inviting them to rubber-stamp. This report builds on *Knowledge graphs for domain assistants* (the KG as verification substrate, hybrid retrieval, the Reviewer agent design) and *Domain assistants for emerging cross domains* (plural dated definitions, versioning, the question router, the evaluation harness). It does not repeat those designs; it specialises them for platform engineering.

## Eleven verdicts: strongest on taxonomy and synonyms, weakest on measured consultation savings

"Evidence strength" rates evidence that the *proposed remedy* works in this setting, not evidence that the problem exists. The problem itself is well documented. Half of developers lose more than 10 hours a week to organisational inefficiency, with finding information the top drain ([Atlassian State of DevEx 2025](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/developer/developer-experience-report-2025)). Over 60% spend at least 30 minutes a day searching for answers, including asking colleagues ([Stack Overflow 2024](https://stackoverflow.blog/2024/09/23/where-developers-feel-ai-coding-tools-are-working-and-where-they-re-missing-the-mark)). DORA associates high-quality documentation with **25% higher team performance** ([DORA](https://dora.dev/capabilities/documentation-quality/)).

| Goal or feature | Verdict | Evidence strength | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1. | F3. Relations among platforms, services, teams, resource kinds | **Yes**: focused ego networks under ~30 nodes; matrix views for governance | Moderate | Hairball views; over-trust in polished graphs |

| F5. Same visuals for governance and for quick explanation | **Yes**, via shared view templates with role-specific overlays | Weak (one qualitative research design, SHACLens; no concept-card studies) | Clutter for product teams; badges leaking governance noise |
| F6. Standard formats (OWL, RDF, Turtle, JSON-LD, SKOS, SHACL) | **Yes**; RDF-native for the semantic layer, JSON-LD contexts over catalog JSON | Strong (W3C standards, mature OSS) | No official RDF for Backstage, TOSCA or ArchiMate; mappings are yours |
| F7. Product teams build their own glossary, taxonomy, ontology, KG | **Yes, as a lightweight template repo** importing the platform core, edited via spreadsheets or YAML | Weak (analogy to OBO/ODK federation; no enterprise case) | Low appetite; product teams will not learn OWL |

The pattern across these verdicts matches the earlier reports. Structure is cheapest and most valuable where it governs *meaning*: canonical terms, kinds, part-whole structure and ownership. It is least proven where it promises *outcomes*, such as consultation hours saved. The rollout plan therefore puts measurement first.

## Platform domains are half-modelled already, which changes the build

Customer manuals forced the earlier design to extract structure from prose, with term-extraction recall as low as 0.30. Platform domains are different because the portals already model the landscape as a typed graph. Backstage describes its catalog as entities (nodes) joined by relations (edges). The kinds are Domain, System, Component, API and Resource, with Group and User for ownership ([Backstage system model](https://backstage.io/docs/features/software-catalog/system-model)). The well-known relation pairs are `ownedBy`, `providesApi`, `consumesApi`, `dependsOn`, `partOf`, `memberOf` and `parentOf` ([Backstage relations](https://backstage.io/docs/features/software-catalog/well-known-relations)). Port, Cortex and OpsLevel offer the same property-graph idea with customisable schemas. Since mid-2025, every one of them exposes the graph to agents over MCP ([Port](https://docs.port.io/ai-interfaces/overview); [OpsLevel](https://opslevel.com/resources/opslevels-new-mcp-server-powers-your-ai-assistant-with-real-time-context); [Cortex](https://www.cortex.io/post/mcp-server)). Below the portal, the infrastructure is described in schemas. Terraform's `providers schema -json` emits every resource type with typed attributes ([HashiCorp](https://developer.hashicorp.com/terraform/cli/commands/providers/schema)). Crossplane XRDs define composite resource kinds, and their Compositions are now function pipelines that declare which resources make up the whole ([Crossplane v2.1](https://docs.crossplane.io/v2.1/composition/compositions/)). Kubernetes CRDs carry OpenAPI schemas, which a CNCF TAG Network issue already calls a latent taxonomy ([cncf/tag-network#39](https://github.com/cncf/tag-network/issues/39)).

What the portals lack is exactly what the stated problems need. They have no class hierarchies, so "a CloudSQL instance and an RDS instance are both managed relational databases" cannot be expressed. They have no SKOS-style concepts or synonyms, no provenance, and no notion of a *capability* that several services realise. That last gap is what lets overlapping offerings hide. None publishes an RDF/OWL export, and Backstage says plainly that its catalog is a cache rather than the source of truth ([Backstage](https://backstage.io/docs/features/software-catalog/creating-the-catalog-graph)). The KG-backed assistant is also an unoccupied niche. Among published internal assistants, only LinkedIn's SQL Bot clearly uses a knowledge graph, and it covers data tables, not platform services ([arXiv 2507.14372](https://prereview.org/preprints/doi-10.48550-arxiv.2507.14372)). The others combine RAG over docs and Slack history with live catalog or platform APIs.

Two consequences shape the design. First, the earlier report's warning that automatic construction yields only candidates applies to *prose-derived* artifacts: definitions, synonyms found in docs, contradictions. Schema-derived kinds, compositions and ownership edges are high-precision inputs, much like the manuals' tables of contents were in the first report. Second, the assistant's *answer accuracy* will come mostly from hybrid retrieval plus live catalog facts, which is the proven pattern. The semantic layer's distinctive value is the one the first report identified: verification and governance. It finds the label collisions, overlapping services, orphan kinds and stale pages that make platform docs contradictory in the first place.

This departs from the first report on one point. That report defaulted to a property graph with RDF export, because its facts came from LLM extraction over prose. Here, the requirement for standard formats, the maturity of SHACL and SKOS tooling, and the fact that sources are already structured make an **RDF-native semantic layer** the simpler choice. Backstage, or another portal, stays the operational catalog that developers edit. The RDF layer adds meaning on top through a hand-written JSON-LD `@context` over entity JSON, which is the lowest-effort mapping available ([Backstage relations](https://backstage.io/docs/features/software-catalog/well-known-relations)). The relation names should stay Backstage's, because product teams already understand `dependsOn` and `ownedBy`.

### The data model reuses W3C vocabularies and adds five platform-specific ideas

No standard ontology covers an internal developer platform, so the core is thin and enterprise-owned, aligned to stable vocabularies. TOSCA 2.0, an OASIS Standard since September 2025, and ArchiMate serve as conceptual references, not imports, because neither has an official RDF serialization ([OASIS](https://www.oasis-open.org/?p=13225); [Open Group](https://pubs.opengroup.org/architecture/archimate31-exchange-file-format-guide)). gist 14 is a maintained upper ontology with a reworked "composites and components" pattern, for teams that want upper-level alignment ([Semantic Arts](https://www.semanticarts.com/gist/)).

| Concept | Modelled as | Reused standard | Harvested from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform team | `core:PlatformTeam ⊑ org:OrganizationalUnit`; time-bounded membership via `org:Membership` | W3C ORG | Backstage Group |
| Platform | `core:Platform`, `ownedBy` a team, offers services | Backstage Domain/System semantics | Backstage Domain/System |
| Capability | Abstract `owl:Class` punned as `skos:Concept` ("relational storage", "secrets management") | SKOS + OWL 2 punning | Curated |
| Service | `core:Service` with stable IRI, `dcat:hasVersion`, `ownedBy`, `core:realizes` a capability | DCAT 3, ArchiMate Realization idea | Backstage Component/System |
| API | `dcat:DataService` / `schema:WebAPI` with `dcat:endpointDescription` → OpenAPI URL; data shapes via schema-automator | DCAT 3 ([arXiv 2303.08883](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.08883)) | Backstage API, OpenAPI |
| Resource kind | OWL class hierarchy (`:PostgresDatabase ⊑ :ManagedRelationalDatabase ⊑ :PlatformResource`), punned as SKOS concepts with `skos:broader` generated from `subClassOf` in CI | OWL, SKOS | CRDs, XRDs, Terraform types, via curated mapping table |
| Compound resource | `:hasComponent ⊑ :hasPart` (transitive) plus non-transitive `:hasDirectComponent`; class-level SHACL shapes such as "a WebAppStack has exactly one Ingress and at least one Deployment" | W3C SWBP part-whole pattern ([W3C](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-swbp-wg/2005Apr/att-0056/simplePartOfWithIndividuals.html)) | Composition pipelines |
| Dependency | Non-transitive `dependsOn`, `providesApi`, `consumesApi`, `hostedOn`; transitive super-property or `dependsOn+` path for blast radius | Backstage relations, TOSCA relationship types | Backstage, cluster state |
| Term | `skos:Concept` with one `prefLabel` per language, team/legacy names as `altLabel`, slang and misspellings as `hiddenLabel`; SKOS-XL only when labels need owner or "deprecated since" metadata | SKOS, SKOS-XL ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/2006/07/SWD/wiki/SkosDesign/SKOS-XL)) | Glossary, query logs, docs |
| Doc page | `core:DocPage` with Diátaxis type, owner, last-verified date, `core:mentions` entities | Dublin Core | TechDocs front matter |
| Journey | Ordered steps → capabilities → owning platform → canonical pages | Custom | Golden-path tutorials |
| Provenance and lifecycle | `prov:wasGeneratedBy` harvest run, `prov:wasDerivedFrom` source; `owl:deprecated` + `dct:isReplacedBy`; `owl:versionIRI` per release | PROV-O, OWL | All |

Three of these choices do most of the work against the stated problems. The **capability-versus-service split** turns "overlapping services" into a query: any capability with more than one realising service is a governance item, either a legitimate alternative to document or a duplicate to retire. The **curated kind-mapping table** is where cross-cloud and cross-team synonyms are resolved. `aws_db_instance`, `XPostgreSQLInstance` and `CloudSQLInstance` become one canonical concept, and the provider names become altLabels. The **page-to-entity links** let a schema change find the prose it invalidates. A minimal Turtle sketch shows the shape:

```turtle
:ManagedRelationalDatabase a owl:Class, skos:Concept ;
    rdfs:subClassOf :PlatformResource ;
    skos:prefLabel "managed relational database"@en ;
    skos:altLabel "DBaaS"@en, "CloudSQL instance"@en ;
    skos:definition "A relational database provisioned and operated by a platform team."@en ;
    core:realizes :RelationalStorageCapability .

:XWebAppStack rdfs:subClassOf :CompoundResource ;
    core:hasDirectComponent :Deployment, :Ingress, :ManagedRelationalDatabase .

:db-platform-postgres a core:Service ;
    core:realizes :RelationalStorageCapability ;
    core:ownedBy :team-data-platform ;
    prov:wasDerivedFrom <backstage:component:default/postgres-service> .
```

The definition records, contested status and validity windows from *Domain assistants for emerging cross domains* apply directly. They matter here because platform terms change meaning quickly ("environment", "tenant", "workspace" and "cluster" often mean different things on different platforms). Each meaning becomes a scoped definition record attributed to its platform, not a silent overwrite.

## Harvest-propose-review is the semi-automatic toolchain for both platform and product teams

The construction process makes machines propose and people approve, through pull requests. Harvest jobs run on a schedule and on source changes, and they open *bot PRs* proposing new kinds, labels, components and ownership edges. Humans curate the one thing harvesting cannot decide: which canonical concept a new kind maps to. LinkML is the hub. Its schema-automator imports JSON Schema (and so OpenAPI component schemas and CRD `openAPIV3Schema`) and SHACL. It imports OWL only in functional syntax after a ROBOT conversion. The project itself warns that seeding is heuristic and loses information from less expressive sources ([schema-automator](https://linkml.io/schema-automator/packages/importers.html); [LinkML FAQ](https://linkml.io/linkml/faq/tools.html)). That is why every generated artifact is a proposal.

Pull-request CI is the quality gate: `linkml-lint`, then the generators; RDF parsing; `robot reason` for consistency and `robot report` for missing labels and definitions; pySHACL against a VocPub-style SKOS profile plus custom rules (one prefLabel per language, no label collisions across concepts, every term has a definition and an owner, team modules only add classes under core classes); and `robot diff -f markdown` posted as a PR comment, the way the Ontology Development Kit already does ([ODK changelog](https://github.stncp.dns-dynamic.net/INCATools/ontology-development-kit/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md); [OBO Academy](https://oboacademy.github.io/obook/howto/github-actions)). On merge, the release receives an `owl:versionIRI`, documentation is published with Widoco or pyLODE for OWL and Skosmos or Prez for SKOS, and the store is reloaded. The same release regenerates the **Vale vocabulary package** for all doc repositories. Preferred terms go to `accept.txt`, banned terms to `reject.txt`, and deprecated synonyms become `substitution` rules that suggest the replacement. A plain reject list cannot express "use X instead of Y" ([Vale](https://vale.sh/docs/keys/vocab)). One merged glossary decision thus changes the assistant's synonym expansion, the docs linter and the visual views at once. This is the "computational" half of federated computational governance ([Fowler/Dehghani](https://martinfowler.com/articles/data-mesh-principles.html)).

Non-ontologists never hand-write Turtle. Platform engineers edit LinkML YAML or Backstage-style YAML they already know. Terminology stewards and product teams edit spreadsheets that voc4cat converts to and from SKOS Turtle, contributed by PR ([voc4cat](https://pypi.org/p/voc4cat)). For prose-derived candidates (definitions in existing manuals, synonyms in Slack history), the earlier reports' pipeline applies unchanged: LLM extraction unioned with statistical term extraction, canonicalisation, provenance spans, and review. The richest synonym-discovery corpus is the assistant's own query log. Unrecognised terms that recur in questions are the synonyms the glossary is missing.

**Product teams** get a cookiecutter of the same repository. It contains a `vocab/` spreadsheet, a small LinkML module that imports the platform core, and the same CI. Their domain terms ("order", "settlement", "tenant") can then reference platform kinds and services by IRI, and their own assistant or docs inherit the platform glossary. The scope should stay deliberately modest: a glossary plus a shallow taxonomy, with ontology classes only where they type real instances. Evidence for this federated shape comes by analogy. The OBO community runs it at scale, with each ontology owning its namespace and importing extracted modules from others ([ODK paper](https://export.arxiv.org/abs/2207.02056)). No enterprise platform-engineering case study was found, so product-team uptake is a pilot hypothesis, not an established result.

## Five view templates serve governance and explanation from the same subgraphs

The visualization evidence sets clear constraints. Edge crossings are the main readability killer ([Purchase](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/BFb0021827)). People struggle with path tasks on dense node-link graphs above about 50 nodes, and on sparse ones above about 100 ([Yoghourdjian et al.](https://deepai.org/publication/scalability-of-network-visualisation-from-a-cognitive-load-perspective)). In ontology work, indented lists support *search* while graphs support *understanding relations* ([Fu, Noy & Storey](https://www.semantic-web-journal.net/node/861)). For software documentation, diagrams are not reliably better than text: in one study, text-first readers scored better even on topology questions ([Heijstek et al.](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A2920847/view)). The first report found that novices prefer trees. The second found that polished graphs caused practitioners to trust wrong answers (LinkQ). The conclusion for busy product teams is a short text answer plus a small, focused diagram. A big graph is the wrong tool.

The design uses one set of SPARQL-selected view templates for both audiences. Platform teams see quality overlays on top.

| View | Question it answers | Product-team rendering | Governance overlay |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kind taxonomy | "What kind of thing is X, and what are its siblings?" | Breadcrumb plus collapsible indented tree (`rdfs:subClassOf*`) | Badges per node: SHACL violations, missing definition, orphan, label collision, staleness colour from last-modified date; green/amber/red diff colouring per PR |
| Compound resource | "What is inside an X stack?" | Part-of tree or Cytoscape compound node, at most ~15 nodes | Shape conformance: required components missing in instances; composition changes |
| Relations ego network | "Who owns X, what does it depend on, which platform offers it?" | 1–2 hop neighbourhood, hierarchical (dagre) layout, answer path highlighted, capped at ~30 nodes with "+N more" aggregate nodes | Unowned nodes, dependencies on deprecated services, cross-platform edges |
| Capability overlap matrix | "Which services realise the same capability?" | Not shown; product teams instead get "for relational storage, use A unless B" | Capabilities × services matrix; rows with more than one realiser flagged. Matrices are reasonable for dense audits ([Ghoniem et al.](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/2357924.htm)) |
| Synonym cluster | "Are X and Y the same thing?" | "Also called…" and "Don't confuse with…" lines on the concept card | prefLabel/altLabel/exactMatch candidates grouped as compound nodes; cross-cluster collisions in red |

Each answer about a concept carries a **concept card**, an unvalidated design inferred from the evidence above. It holds the name, a type badge (resource kind, service, platform, capability) and the owner team. Then come a definition of at most two sentences, the broader-chain breadcrumb, a mini diagram of no more than 15 nodes, synonyms, a "don't confuse with" line built from siblings and near-synonyms, and links to the canonical how-to and reference pages. Governance users also see last-verified dates and open issues. Every element is generated from the KG by SPARQL; the LLM only paraphrases. Diagrams must be produced **deterministically**: SPARQL CONSTRUCT, then a serialiser to Mermaid or DOT, validated by a parser before sending. The only formal benchmark of LLM-written Mermaid found significant capability gaps between models ([MermaidSeqBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14967)). LinkML's `gen-erdiagram -c` already gives a deterministic "class plus reachable classes" view for schema documentation ([LinkML](https://linkml.io/linkml/generators/erdiagram.html)). Inferred edges (from transitive closure) should be drawn differently from asserted ones, so the picture cannot overstate what the sources say.

Tooling follows cost. If the enterprise runs Backstage, the catalog-graph plugin is the cheapest delivery path. It supports custom relation pairs through `ALL_RELATION_PAIRS` or the community relations plugin, so `realizes`, `hasComponent` and `instanceOfKind` can appear on entity pages without a new UI ([Backstage catalog-graph](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@backstage/plugin-catalog-graph@0.6.6/README.md); [dweber019 plugin](https://www.npmjs.com/package/%40dweber019%2Fbackstage-plugin-relations)). For the governance workbench, Cytoscape.js (MIT; compound nodes; level-of-detail switching at 4,000 elements) covers module-sized views ([Cytoscape.js](https://github.com/cytoscape/cytoscape.js/blob/e6541de603f5714f572238573ea06ef17503282b/documentation/md/core/init.md)). Sigma.js handles large overviews ([Sigma](https://v4.sigmajs.org/concepts/rendering/)). Reactodia, the maintained LGPL fork of Ontodia, provides RDF-native exploration and editing ([Reactodia](https://reactodia.github.io/docs)). Avoid Neo4j NVL unless Neo4j is licensed, because its license terms are unclear for other stores ([npm](https://npmjs.com/package/%40neo4j-nvl%2Fbase)). The quality workbench should copy the coordinated-views design of SHACLens: class tree with violation counts, a filterable table as the main work surface, node-link drill-down for the selection, and an LLM that explains violations and drafts fixes for human review ([SHACLens](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioinformatics/articles/10.3389/fbinf.2026.1756507/full)). SHACLens was evaluated only qualitatively, and no mature off-the-shelf *visual* OWL/SKOS diff exists, so the PR diff view is custom work on top of ROBOT diff ([ROBOT](https://robot.obolibrary.org/diff)).

## Federated governance makes terminology a CI check, not a meeting

The operating model adapts data mesh's federated computational governance. Teams own their content, and a small federated body sets standards that the platform enforces automatically ([Fowler/Dehghani](https://martinfowler.com/articles/data-mesh-principles.html)). Practitioner evidence points to two failure modes on opposite sides. Capital One built a federated contribution framework for its enterprise ontology because central governance became a bottleneck ([SEMANTiCS 2022](https://2022-eu.semantics.cc/node/3655)). Spotify found that dedicated writers for Golden Path tutorials did not scale, and that moving ownership to many teams "creates coordination gaps" ([Spotify Engineering](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2020/8/how-we-use-golden-paths-to-solve-fragmentation-in-our-software-ecosystem)). The design answer is a small central core with automated rules, broad team ownership of everything else, and a *named owner for every cross-platform journey*.

| Role | Owns | Decides |
|---|---|---|
| Semantic council (architecture lead, KG owner, 1–2 rotating platform representatives) | Core namespaces, upper classes, relation vocabulary, SHACL profiles, capability taxonomy | Core changes, cross-platform term disputes, capability-overlap rulings; SLA on decisions to avoid becoming the bottleneck |
| Platform team (one terminology steward each) | Its module under `platform/<team>/`, local terms, docs (CODEOWNERS), freshness SLA, mapping of its kinds to canonical concepts | Team-module PRs once CI is green |
| DevEx/docs enabling team | Portal, information architecture, Diátaxis templates, Vale package, assistant, gap triage, view templates | Page-type standards, assistant releases |
| Journey owners | One end-to-end golden-path tutorial each, linking into platform-owned pages | Narrative and step order; files issues against platforms |
| Product-team steward (optional) | Team glossary/KG repository importing the core | Local terms; proposes promotion of shared terms to the core |
| Reviewer AI | Nothing; it proposes, prioritises, explains with verified evidence | Never commits |

The documentation side follows the docs-as-code baseline that has the most practitioner support. Markdown lives in owning repositories under CODEOWNERS and is rendered centrally (TechDocs). Every page declares a Diátaxis type, owner and last-verified date in front matter. Google's experience is that unowned documents go stale ([SWE at Google ch. 10](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch10.html)). The portal's top-level navigation is organised by product-team journey, and the second level by platform. Contradictions and staleness are attacked from machine-readable truth outward. Reference pages are generated from OpenAPI, CRD and Terraform schemas, so they cannot drift. Each schema diff, for example an oasdiff run in CI ([oasdiff](https://github.com/tufin/oasdiff/blob/v1.10.16/BREAKING-CHANGES.md)), uses the page-to-entity links to open "review needed" tasks on the how-to and explanation pages that mention changed entities. Reference scanning in the style of Tan et al. finds pages naming removed elements ([arXiv 2212.01479](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.01479)). LLM consistency checking runs only as triage. Cross-platform contradictions ("platform A's page says the default quota is X, platform B's says Y") follow the first report's formalise-then-reason method: extract typed facts with their scope, generate candidates from SPARQL rules, and verify each against source passages.

The **Reviewer AI** keeps the first report's safeguards: queue state in a database, evidence checked by string match, sessions well under 100 items, decide-before-reveal for high-impact items, and a second approver for merges and re-parenting. It gains platform-specific queues, ordered roughly by consultation impact:

- **Synonym collisions.** New labels that collide with existing prefLabels or altLabels, and query-log terms with no glossary match.
- **Kind mapping.** Harvested kinds with no canonical mapping.
- **Capability overlap.** Capabilities with several realising services.
- **Orphans and ownership gaps.** Orphan or unowned services and kinds.
- **Stale references.** Doc pages that mention deprecated or changed entities.
- **Contradiction candidates.** Contradictions between platforms' docs.
- **Gap clusters.** Weekly gap clusters from the assistant, routed to the owning platform via the KG.

The measured human-factors risks remain the binding constraint. Reviewers given wrong LLM suggestions lost accuracy ([arXiv 2507.14552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.14552v1)), and requiring correction of flagged AI errors *increased* acceptance of wrong suggestions in a preregistered study with N=2,784 ([arXiv 2509.08514](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08514v1)). The Reviewer's success metric is therefore caught errors and cross-release consistency, not acceptance rate.

Incentives need explicit design. DORA 2023 linked documentation work to burnout among respondents from underrepresented groups, plausibly because they carry a disproportionate share of it ([DX newsletter](https://newsletter.getdx.com/p/2023-dora-report)). Doc health and gap closure should therefore appear on per-team portal scorecards and count as roadmap work, with the load spread deliberately.

## Measure consultations against a control, and roll out in four gated phases

### Metrics separate outcomes from assistant health and knowledge hygiene

Vendor deflection ratios (deflected conversations over total conversations) overstate impact, and definitions vary widely between case studies ([Tray.ai/Apollo](https://www.casestudies.com/company/tray-ai/case-study/apollo-deflects-40-of-it-tickets-with-trayai)). Glean's widely quoted 20% reduction in support requests rests on a vendor-commissioned study of four customers ([Forrester TEI](https://tei.forrester.com/go/Glean/workAIplatform/)). The program must therefore establish its own baseline and use a design that supports counterfactual comparison: roll out to product teams in staggered waves and compare them with teams that have not yet started.

| Tier | Metric | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outcome | Consultation requests and hours per product team per month | Booking or ticket system | Primary metric; staggered rollout as control |
| Outcome | Share of consultation time spent on "routine" vs "design" questions | Session tagging by consultants | Tests whether demand shifts rather than shrinks |
| Outcome | Time to resolution for platform questions | Support channel | Spotify-style metric; easier to move than volume |
| Outcome | Time to first successful integration on a golden path | Pipeline events | Journey-level effect |
| Outcome | DORA doc-quality items (findable, accurate, useful), quarterly | Survey | Matches the three stated doc problems ([DORA](https://dora.dev/capabilities/documentation-quality/)) |
| Assistant | Helpfulness or acceptance rate; escalation rate; negative sentiment | Assistant logs | GoDaddy's and Uber's metrics; their 48.9% and 80% figures bracket expectations |
| Assistant | "Did this answer stop you from booking a consultation?" | End-of-session prompt | Direct but self-reported deflection |
| Assistant | Correct abstention on fabricated or unknown terms; low-confidence rate per platform | Probe set, logs | Fabricated-term probes from the second report |
| Assistant | Gap-cluster closure time | Backlog | Loop health |
| Knowledge | Share of kinds and services with definition, owner, capability mapping | SPARQL | Hygiene |
| Knowledge | Open label collisions; open capability overlaps; SHACL violations | CI, issue store | Synonym and overlap reduction |
| Knowledge | Share of pages within freshness SLA; open drift alerts; Vale violations per repo | CI | Alignment progress |
| Reviewer | Reviewer-caught error rate; rubber-stamp indicators (decision time, acceptance by item type) | Review log | Guards against automation bias |

A defensible planning hypothesis is a moderate reduction in *routine* consultation requests, with larger gains in resolution time. It sits between Elastic's 7% and the self-reported range of internal assistants. This is an inference, not a benchmark, and the pilot exists to replace it with a measured number.

### The rollout starts where consultations cluster

| Phase | Duration | Scope | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Baseline | 4–6 weeks | Instrument consultation requests. Tag the last quarter's sessions by question type, platform and terms used. Pick 1–2 high-friction cross-platform journeys and the 3–5 platforms they cross. Build a gold question set from real consultation and support transcripts, including unanswerable, ambiguous and contradictory-source items | Baseline numbers per product team; gold set of a few hundred items; named journey owners |
| 1. Spine | ~3 months | LinkML core; SKOS glossary seeded from catalog, docs and transcripts; harvesters for Backstage plus CRD/XRD or Terraform for the pilot platforms; SHACL and CI; Vale package; taxonomy tree, concept card and ego views. Assistant in shadow mode: hybrid retrieval, glossary normalisation, catalog/KG facts via MCP; consultants see its draft answers | CI green on core; collision and orphan counts known; shadow answers rated by consultants against the gold set |
| 2. Pilot | ~3 months | Assistant live in the support channel for 3–5 product teams in staggered waves, with escalation that pre-fills consultation tickets. Reviewer queues running weekly. Gap loop to platform backlogs. Compound-resource and capability-overlap views. One rewritten golden-path tutorial | Pilot teams' routine consultation requests fall measurably versus not-yet-onboarded teams; helpfulness at or above Uber's ~49%; fabricated-term abstention passes; no rise in incorrect-advice escalations |
| 3. Scale | 6–12 months | All platforms onboard modules; capability-overlap rulings by the council; schema-diff drift triggers; product-team template repository; journey scorecards. Decide whether the assistant becomes the *default* first contact only after an explicit experiment, as GoDaddy did | Hygiene metrics trending; at least one product-team KG importing the core; survey doc-quality scores up |
| 4. Optimise | Ongoing | Run the second report's factorial harness: graph traversal vs templates vs plain hybrid, by question class. Consider GraphRAG-style summaries only if global "what does our platform estate offer for X" questions prove frequent | Configuration choices backed by paired, per-class results |

Two sequencing choices follow from the evidence. The glossary, kind taxonomy and catalog grounding come first, because they address synonyms and "which platform" questions at vector-RAG cost. DORA 2024 also found that platforms mandated for the whole lifecycle were associated with **about 8% lower throughput and 14% lower change stability** ([DORA 2024](https://dora.dev/research/2024/2024-DORA-Report-Infographic.pdf)). That argues against forcing the assistant on teams as the only channel before an experiment shows it works.

## Risks cluster around trust, staleness and governance politics

The main risks have known mitigations, but each needs an owner. **Stale facts** come first. Backstage is a cache, and the assistant cannot see upstream outages. GoDaddy's assistant was itself down twice in a year ([GoDaddy](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/news/how-we-scaled-platform-support-10x-without-scaling-the-team)). Mitigations are live API grounding for fast-changing facts, harvest provenance with timestamps on every fact, and a fallback route to humans. **Trust** is fragile. One summary of the Stack Overflow 2025 survey puts trust in AI accuracy at 33% ([SO 2025](https://stackoverflow.blog/2025/08/01/diving-into-the-results-of-the-2025-developer-survey/)), and Uber's rework was driven by incorrect advice on security questions ([Uber](https://www.uber.com/en-AU/blog/enhanced-agentic-rag/)). Every answer must cite owned, dated sources, and high-stakes or low-confidence questions must be routed to humans while still being logged as gaps. **Governance politics** cut both ways: a council that is too central becomes a bottleneck, one that is too loose lets synonyms multiply. Decision SLAs and CI-enforced rules keep it moving. **Synonym expansion** that is too broad causes query drift, so expansion must be scoped by platform, as the first report noted. **Custom extractors** are ongoing maintenance, especially as Crossplane's composition model keeps changing. Some field names used for instance harvesting were not verified in this research. **Visual over-trust** and **reviewer automation bias** are human-factors risks already measured in the earlier reports. **Permission leakage** applies wherever docs and Slack history have differing access rights; Glean's experience is that answers are only as good as connector permission coverage ([Glean](https://www.glean.com/product/enterprise-graph)). **Ontology over-engineering** is the quiet risk. Product teams will not read OWL, so every artifact must earn its place by powering a view, a lint rule or an answer.

Several questions remain open and should be settled during Phase 0 or tracked through the pilot:

- **System of record.** Is ownership mastered in the portal, in HR or org data, or in the RDF layer? The design assumes the portal, with the RDF layer derived.
- **Capability ownership.** Who owns the capability taxonomy when platform teams disagree about overlap? Should overlap rulings ever force a service to retire?
- **Does the graph add answer accuracy?** No source measures how much KG or catalog context improves an internal assistant's accuracy beyond retrieval over docs plus the catalog API. This should be tested directly in Phase 4.
- **Does consultation demand shrink or move?** Consultation demand may move from routine questions to design reviews rather than shrink. The tagging metric exists to detect this.
- **Product-team appetite.** How much appetite do product teams have for their own glossaries and KGs, and is a glossary plus shallow taxonomy enough?
- **Concept cards and short diagrams.** No study has tested concept cards or short diagrams in chat assistants. The 15–30 node caps are extrapolated from graph-readability research.
- **Unverified tool details.** Current releases of pySHACL, Oxigraph, Fuseki, Skosmos and ROBOT, SSSOM for mapping governance, and Humanitec's resource-graph model were not verified in this research.

## Conclusion

The platform setting reverses the economics of the earlier reports. For customer manuals, structure had to be extracted from prose at low recall, so the knowledge graph earned its place mainly as a verification substrate. Inside an enterprise, most of the structure already exists in the catalog and infrastructure schemas. The cheapest, highest-leverage move is therefore not better extraction but *reconciliation*: one canonical name per concept, one place per resource kind in a hierarchy, and one explicit statement of which services realise which capability. Many consultations exist because these three things are implicit. The assistant answers routine questions, but the larger long-term effect is that the same graph surfaces the overlaps, collisions and stale pages that generate those questions. It routes each to the team that can remove it. Success should therefore be judged by whether the *rate at which confusing platform knowledge is created* falls, not only by deflected questions.

The second implication concerns where the real work sits. The standards and tools are ready, and the novel engineering is modest: a few extractors, a JSON-LD context, view templates and CI rules. The hard parts are a council that decides quickly, journey owners who have authority across team boundaries, and reviewers who stay sceptical of a helpful AI. An enterprise that funds those roles and measures consultations against a staggered control will produce something no published source yet has. That is an independently measured result for whether a governed platform semantic layer reduces consultation load.
