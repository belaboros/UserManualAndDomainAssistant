# Structured knowledge helps, but only behind evidence

Yes, but not in the way GraphRAG marketing suggests. Glossaries and a small, schema-bound ontology will clearly help all three journeys. Knowledge graphs help answer quality only as an *add-on* to hybrid chunk retrieval. Their bigger payoff is as the backbone for detecting quality issues and for structured review. A Reviewer AI assistant that drives an interactive governance process is feasible with today's components. No one has yet built and measured the full proactive, multi-session version, and its main risks are about how people behave, not about the technology. The answer-quality evidence is sobering. Independent benchmarks show graph retrieval beating plain vector RAG only on multi-hop, comparison and temporal questions, usually by a few points. Graphs lose on the single-hop, procedural and definitional questions that dominate manual Q&A. They collapse correct "not documented" answers when global community summaries drive the response, and triplet-only retrieval loses 20–35 F1 points ([Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v2); [Xiang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690v2)). Automatic construction gives *candidates*, not finished models. Term extraction reaches about 0.6–0.7 F1 in engineering domains, and is-a hierarchy induction often stays below 0.5 F1 ([arXiv 2508.19428](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19428v1)). That is exactly why a human review loop is needed, and why the Reviewer assistant is the part of the design that makes the domain model trustworthy. The winning architecture pairs deterministic structure extraction from the manuals with a seeded schema. It uses LLM extraction plus canonicalization, SHACL and rule-based validators, and hybrid retrieval with graph context attached to source text. A review agent that proposes, explains with verified evidence, and never commits is the governance layer on top.

## Verdicts: strong yes for glossaries, conditional yes for graphs

The three journeys draw on the structured artifacts in different ways, and the evidence behind each differs a lot in strength. The table gives the bottom line. The sections that follow give the reasoning.

| Journey / question | Verdict | Evidence strength | Dominant risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Downloader builds indexes, glossary, ontology and KG | **Yes, as candidate generation plus deterministic structure.** Not as an autonomous model builder | Moderate: shared-task benchmarks (LLMs4OL, Text2KGBench), but none on user manuals | Low recall (R≈0.30 on engineering terms), hallucinated relations (~17%), over- and under-merging across versions |
| 1. Downloader collects quality issues | **Yes for deterministic checks** (undefined terms, SHACL gaps, deprecated terms, version supersession). **Partial for contradictions** (~64% recall, ~31% false flags with frontier LLMs) | Moderate for contradiction detection, weak for gaps, drift and staleness | Reviewers flooded with false positives; "contradictions" that are really differences in version or variant scope |
| 2. Q&A answers like an expert | **Glossary: strong yes. Ontology: yes, for disambiguation and scoping. KG: modest gains only on multi-hop, comparison and temporal questions, and only integrated with chunk retrieval** | Strong (independent benchmarks) for general QA; weak for manuals specifically | Graph-only retrieval, global summaries that break abstention, context and cost blow-up |
| 2. Q&A surfaces quality issues | **Yes**, provided issues come from a pre-computed store rather than in-context detection alone | Moderate (CONFLICTS, WikiContradict); no UX studies on manuals | LLMs omitting a conflict or arbitrarily picking one side; alert fatigue |
| 3. Reviewer AI drives interactive governance | **Feasible and differentiated, but unproven end-to-end.** Every component has precedent; the combination does not | Weak to moderate: small user studies (N≈3–24), vendor features without metrics | Automation bias, sycophancy, reviewer fatigue past ~100 items, hallucinated evidence |

"As close to a real human expert as possible" is best read as a behavioural target, not a retrieval target. Experts ask "which model and firmware?", define terms precisely, reason across dependencies, say "the manuals don't cover this", and warn when two documents disagree. Default RAG systems do none of this reliably. On the joint Answer/Ask/Abstain decision, standard RAG reaches only **26.7% decision accuracy**, and improved methods plateau at 34–38% ([arXiv 2605.25831](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25831)). The glossary and ontology matter most because they make several of these behaviours possible. Version and variant entities drive clarifying questions, glossary entries ground definitions, and relations support dependency reasoning. The issue store drives the warnings. The KG's contribution to raw answer accuracy is the smallest of the three.

## Graph retrieval adds a few points on a minority of questions

The most rigorous independent comparison, Han et al.'s systematic evaluation, shows no graph method dominating. On single-hop NQ, plain RAG scored **68.18 F1** against 65.44 for Community-GraphRAG Local and **28.50 for triplets-only KG retrieval**. On multi-hop HotpotQA the best graph method gained only about one point ([Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v2)). The big graph wins were on specific question types. On MultiHop-RAG, temporal questions jumped from **25.73 to 49.06** with Community Local and comparison questions rose about four points. On null queries, where the right answer is "insufficient information", global community search fell to **13.95% against RAG's 91.36%** ([Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v2)). For a manual assistant whose credibility depends on saying "this is not documented", that one number rules out letting global summaries drive answers. GraphRAG-Bench reinforces the pattern. Reranked vector RAG beat MS-GraphRAG on fact retrieval (60.9 vs 49.3). On the structured medical corpus, the one closest to technical documentation, RAG beat MS-GraphRAG on summarization **65.8 to 41.9**, and graph methods added only about three points on complex reasoning ([Xiang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690v2)).

Two further findings shrink the apparent graph advantage. When vector RAG gets the same token budget as GraphRAG, the gap nearly vanishes (71.01 vs 71.17 on the 70B model). Much of GraphRAG's "win" is simply more context ([Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v2)). Headline win rates from pairwise LLM judges are also fragile. Swapping answer order shifted LightRAG-vs-itself win rates by more than 30%, and after de-biasing, NaiveRAG slightly beat LightRAG instead of losing 28–72 ([Zeng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06331v1)). This undercuts Microsoft's comprehensiveness claims for GraphRAG and LazyGraphRAG, which rest on synthetic queries over a single news corpus judged by an LLM ([Edge et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130); [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazygraphrag-setting-a-new-standard-for-quality-and-cost/)).

The steelman for graphs is complementarity, not replacement. In Han et al., **13.6% of queries were answered correctly only by GraphRAG and 11.6% only by RAG**. *Integrating* both retrievals into one context added **+6.4 points**, whereas routing each query to one retriever added just +1.1 ([Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v2)). Domain studies point the same way, though they are self-reported. Hybrid GraphRAG improved factual correctness by 8% on O-RAN telecom specifications ([Ahmad et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03608)). HybridRAG raised answer relevance from 0.91 to 0.96 on earnings calls, but context precision dropped to 0.79, so the merged context needs reranking ([Sarmah et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.04948v1)). Ontology-guided KGs were competitive with state-of-the-art GraphRAG only when chunk text was attached to the nodes ([da Cruz et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05991)). Costs are real too. MS-GraphRAG local search used about **38.7K prompt tokens per query** against about 879 for vanilla RAG, and graph construction took roughly 40–57× longer than vector indexing ([Xiang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690v2); [Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v2)). The conclusion for journey 2 is clear. Hybrid dense plus BM25 retrieval over chunks is the baseline. Graph traversal is added for comparison, dependency, version and cross-manual questions, always carrying source text, under a bounded token budget.

The glossary is where the evidence and the logic line up best, even though nobody has published a controlled benchmark for it. HybridRAG's graph component failed on questions that did not name an entity explicitly ([Sarmah et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.04948v1)). That makes an alias and acronym table the gateway to graph lookup. An industrial fintech agentic RAG system dedicates a component to glossary-based acronym resolution and fails exactly where acronyms are undefined ([arXiv 2510.25518](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25518v1)). Practitioners at Palo Alto Networks index a separate synonym-expanded embedding for product jargon ([Medium](https://medium.com/@impuneetgupta/bridging-the-language-gap-our-journey-to-a-synonym-aware-rag-system-at-palo-alto-networks-e95c35f40004)). The caveat is query drift: over-broad synonym expansion pulls in unrelated passages ([apxml](https://apxml.com/courses/optimizing-rag-for-production/chapter-2-advanced-retrieval-optimization/query-augmentation-rag)). Expansion must therefore be scoped per product, version and tenant. One governed glossary serves four jobs: query normalization, entity linking, definitional answers, and terminology-drift detection. That makes it the highest-leverage artifact in the whole system.

## Automatic construction yields reviewable candidates, not a domain model

The downloader can build every artifact the brief lists, but the quality varies sharply by task, and this is what justifies journey 3. In the LLMs4OL 2025 challenge, the best Text2Onto F1 on the **Engineering** domain was **0.62 for terms and 0.675 for types**. One strong team reported **precision 0.825 but recall only 0.30** on engineering term extraction, so LLMs are conservative and miss most terms. Taxonomy discovery ranged from **0.12 to 0.66 F1** across ontologies ([arXiv 2508.19428](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19428v1)). Typing terms into a *given* type system is far easier: few-shot term typing reached up to 0.94 F1 on the same leaderboard. So a fixed, human-owned core schema with LLM typing into it beats letting the LLM invent the type system. Schema-constrained triple extraction gives well-formed output, not correct output. Text2KGBench baselines achieved **83% ontology conformance but only 0.35 F1** against gold, with about 17% hallucinated subjects, relations and objects ([arXiv 2308.02357](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.02357)). These are 2023-era model numbers and frontier models will do better, but no 2025–26 head-to-head exists to say by how much.

Canonicalization is the step that keeps a multi-manual, multi-version corpus from splintering into near-duplicates or collapsing homonyms. The best current methods first extract openly, then cluster by embedding and let an LLM rule on merges. EDC writes a definition for each induced type and maps it to the nearest schema element ([arXiv 2404.03868](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03868v1)). KGGen resolves entities and edges separately, with explicit guards against merging distinct concepts, and retained **66.1% of facts against GraphRAG's 47.8%** on its (synthetic) MINE-1 benchmark ([arXiv 2502.09956](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09956v2)). Out of the box, GraphRAG merges only entities with identical title and type ([GraphRAG docs](https://microsoft.github.io/graphrag/index/default_dataflow/)). Neo4j's own guidance calls disambiguation a problem with "no perfect solutions" ([Neo4j](https://neo4j.com/docs/neo4j-graphrag-python/current/user_guide_kg_builder.html)). The literature does not address versions at all. Versioned facts therefore need explicit modelling: a stable concept ID plus version-scoped assertions. Otherwise deduplication will silently fold release differences together.

The highest-precision signal is already in the manuals. Tables of contents, numbered steps, menu paths, cross-references and existing glossary sections can be parsed deterministically. A SunExpress aircraft-maintenance project built its KG entirely from the manuals' existing cross-reference notation, with no LLM at construction time. Its claimed 95% seed-retrieval accuracy is self-reported in social posts only, so treat it as an anecdote rather than evidence ([LinkedIn](https://tr.linkedin.com/in/muhammetduzparmak)). Procedural-KG extraction from maintenance manuals with few-shot prompting is documented at small scale ([arXiv 2310.03376](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03376)). Parsing tools are mature: Docling's TableFormer scores 93.6 on table structure ([Docling models](https://huggingface.co/docling-project/docling-models)). Proposing higher-level concepts remains the weakest link. LLM taxonomy studies find placing inner nodes difficult ([ACL 2025 industry](https://preview.aclanthology.org/landing_page/2025.acl-industry.55.pdf)). GraphRAG's Leiden communities produce *thematic* groupings, not is-a classes ([GraphRAG docs](https://microsoft.github.io/graphrag/index/default_dataflow/)). Abstractions should come from three converging signals: TOC structure, term-embedding clusters and graph communities. They then enter the review queue as proposals. They must not be committed as ontology classes.

On validation, the strongest pattern pairs a constrained schema with deterministic gates. In DOPKG-SHACL ablations, deterministic IRI minting and ontology alignment mattered most, so stable IDs must come from code, not the LLM ([Edge Hill](https://research.edgehill.ac.uk/en/publications/deterministic-and-trustworthy-llm-driven-semantic-knowledge-graph/)). OntoLogX and ANCHOR send SHACL violations back for bounded repair, at most three retries, and reject the output if it cannot be repaired ([arXiv 2510.01409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01409); [arXiv 2606.01208](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01208)). SHACL catches structural errors but not plausible-but-false facts. Provenance from every node and edge back to a source span is therefore the last line of defence for both the reviewer and the answering assistant.

## Quality issues are detectable when facts are formalized first

Detecting gaps, contradictions and terminology drift is the most distinctive promise of the downloader. It is also where pure-LLM approaches are least trustworthy. On FIND, domain experts planted inconsistencies in long technical documents, and the best model (gpt-5) recovered only **64%** of them. On real arXiv papers, **136 of its 196** flags were judged legitimate, so roughly 31% were noise ([arXiv 2512.18601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18601)). ContraDoc found GPT-4 strongest but unreliable on contradictions that need nuance and context. Negation and numeric contradictions were easiest ([arXiv 2311.09182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09182v2)). Plain-LLM doc-vs-code checking produced false positives "high enough to be unusable", mostly from treating normal abstraction gaps as inconsistencies. DocPrism made it usable by capping the flag rate at 15% and adding categorization filters, reaching **0.62 precision** ([arXiv 2511.00215](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00215v1)).

Requirements engineering is the closest analogue to manuals, and it gives the clearest architectural signal: formalize first, then reason. SAT-LLM extracts requirements into SMT constraints and reported **P 1.00 / R 0.83** against plain ChatGPT's **R 0.31** ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10574434)). The logic-based ALICE tool likewise beat an LLM-only baseline ([Cambridge DESIGN 2024](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-design-society/article/towards-an-automatic-contradiction-detection-in-requirements-engineering/C7D7E69A58BBC2BC6054AF0BB972A3E1)). This is the strongest argument for the KG in the whole design. The downloader extracts typed facts such as (setting, value or range, default, applicability conditions, version, source). Contradiction candidates then come from SPARQL or SHACL rules: "same setting, same scope, different default". That replaces quadratic free-text comparison (20 documents already make 190 pairs, per [arXiv 2504.00180](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.00180v1)). An LLM verifies each candidate against the source passages. Capturing applicability conditions is the single biggest false-positive reducer, because many apparent manual contradictions are really version or variant differences.

Gaps and drift map to deterministic checks better than to LLM judgment. A term that is used but never defined, a procedure missing a prerequisite or expected result, or an error code with no resolution can all be expressed as SHACL cardinality constraints. A SHACL post-extraction gate has caught missing links, invalid types and broken references before expert review ([NeurIPS 2026](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/158007)). LLM-prompted completeness detection reached only about **0.3 F1** in one non-peer-reviewed study ([Karaca thesis](https://www.github.com/yavuzkaraca/bachelor-thesis)). Coverage can also be made testable. Generate competency questions from the ontology and from real user queries, run them through the Q&A system, and treat unanswered ones as gap candidates. OE-Assist showed that LLM verification of competency questions matches the average user ([arXiv 2507.14552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14552v1)). Terminology drift is a solved industrial pattern. A termbase marks terms as preferred, admitted or deprecated, any deprecated occurrence is flagged, and "term discovery" clusters variants for a terminologist to approve ([Markup AI/Acrolinx](https://support.markup.ai/hc/en-us/articles/10228296536082)). Normalizing plural and hyphenation variants first matters: an ontology-reuse study found many flagged "synonym" clusters were only such variants ([arXiv 2205.02892](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.02892)).

For journey 2, models do not surface conflicts well on their own. CONFLICTS found LLMs misclassify, omit or arbitrarily pick between conflicting sources. Explicit conflict-reasoning prompts help, but large headroom remains ([arXiv 2506.08500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08500v2)). WikiContradict found implicit conflicts especially hard to reflect ([arXiv 2406.13805](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13805v1)). The assistant should therefore pull known, pre-computed issues attached to the entities and passages it retrieved, rather than rediscover conflicts per query. It should show them only when the question's entities intersect the issue, with both sources, their versions and dates, and the preferred reading with its reason. User studies show trust depends on how clear and actionable an explanation is, not only on answer quality ([arXiv 2601.14460](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14460v1)), and that confidence cues increase source checking ([CEUR Vol-4265](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4265/paper4.pdf)). Staleness needs metadata, not semantics: a chunk describing a deprecated flow can still score about 0.92 similarity to a current question ([tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-05-07-stale-docs-confident-wrong-answers-rag-knowledge-base)). No controlled study yet compares disclosing a disagreement with silently resolving it in documentation QA, so the presentation design should be A/B tested.

## A Reviewer agent is feasible; human factors decide success

Every primitive the Reviewer assistant needs exists somewhere. The industry baseline is per-item accept, edit or reject, bulk actions, and AI-origin flags in an activity log. PoolParty 10.1 offers this ([Graphwise](https://help.graphwise.ai/en/graphwise-documentation/poolparty-10-1---ai-powered-taxonomy-creation.html)), as do Collibra's "Ready for review" tags ([Collibra](https://productresources.collibra.com/docs/collibra/2026.08/Content/BusinessGlossary/to_business-glossary.htm)) and Atlan's activity log ([Atlan](https://atlan.com/demos/use-atlan-ai-document-terms.md)). TopBraid EDG 8.5's "Problems & Suggestions" panel, which surfaces missing and conflicting links, is the nearest vendor analogue to issue review ([TopQuadrant](https://www.topquadrant.com/?p=21009)). ODK-AI runs Claude Code as a pull-request author on ontology repositories, and humans approve each merge ([ODK-AI](http://ai4curation.io/odk-ai)). The closest research analogue is IDEA2. It is an LLM-extract, expert accept/reject/comment, LLM-reformulate loop with JSON-LD provenance, and it reached **92.7% final acceptance at about 0.85 minutes per item** over three iterations ([arXiv 2604.01344](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01344v1)). But IDEA2 reviewed competency questions, not a whole domain model, with 3–4 experts. No product or paper documents a *proactive, multi-session, conversational* agent that reviews terms, relations, abstractions and quality issues together. That gap is white space for differentiation, and it is also the reason the evidence for this journey is weak.

The measured risks are human ones. In OE-Assist, 19 knowledge engineers gained accuracy when LLM suggestions were correct and **lost accuracy when they were wrong**, so overall performance did not change significantly ([arXiv 2507.14552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.14552v1)). A preregistered study with **N=2,784** found that requiring reviewers to correct flagged AI errors *increased* acceptance of incorrect suggestions. Showing system strengths before weaknesses amplified automation bias ([arXiv 2509.08514](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08514v1)). Cognitive forcing designs, where the reviewer decides first and only then sees the AI's view, reduced overreliance, though users liked them least ([arXiv 2102.09692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692v1)). Sycophancy is a general property of RLHF-trained assistants ([arXiv 2310.13548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)). An agent that simply agrees with the admin's corrections would quietly degrade consistency. LLM-generated ontologies contain **significant superfluous elements** ([arXiv 2503.05388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05388v1)), so compactness has to be checked explicitly. IDEA2 flags fatigue beyond about 100 items and found that vague "improve this" feedback produced poor revisions, while free-text rationales drove good ones ([arXiv 2604.01344](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01344v1)). OntoChat users struggled to write prompts, which argues for an agent that drives the session with structured options rather than waiting for instructions ([arXiv 2408.15256](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15256v3)).

Prioritization and visualization have solid, if partial, support. Budgeted KG auditing works. ACTC calibrates per-relation acceptance thresholds from a few labels ([arXiv 2305.06395](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.06395v3.pdf)). KAEL ensembles error detectors and queries experts only on suspicious triples ([WSDM 2023](https://www4.comp.polyu.edu.hk/~xiaohuang/docs/2023WSDM_Junnan.pdf)). Reviewing items from the same source text in succession cuts cost ([Qi & Zheng](https://www.wict.pku.edu.cn/docs/20221201164039326398.pdf)). For diagrams, novices find **indented trees** more organized and familiar, while node-link graphs work better for multiple inheritance ([Fu, Noy & Storey](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-642-41335-3_8.pdf)). ALADDIN uses entity cards rather than whole graphs as the unit of review ([CIKM 2025](https://www.dei.unipd.it/~marches1/papers/ims_etal-cikm-2025.pdf)). The practical design follows from this. Show taxonomies as collapsible trees with proposed changes shown as a diff. Show a concept as a depth-1–2 ego graph. Show a contradiction as the minimal conflicting subgraph with its source snippets. Show an abstraction as a before/after tree. Keep views small, roughly 30–50 nodes. That cap is a heuristic, since no study has measured a threshold.

## Architecture: deterministic spine, LLM proposals, governed commits

The evidence converges on one blueprint. The table maps each component to its evidence. The guiding principle throughout: **detection and identity are deterministic, the LLM proposes and explains, and only a human commits.**

| Layer | Design decision | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition and parsing | Store product, version, edition, language, URL, date and hash per manual; parse with Docling into a DOM that keeps heading paths, tables, figures and pages; chunk by section (≈300–800 tokens) with heading-path prefix | Table fidelity ([Docling](https://huggingface.co/docling-project/docling-models)); larger chunks lower extraction fidelity ([GraphRAG docs](https://microsoft.github.io/graphrag/index/default_dataflow/)) |
| Deterministic structure graph | TOC → section tree, numbered steps → Procedure/Step, menu paths and UI labels, cross-references, existing glossary sections; treated as high-precision ground truth | Is-a induction ≤0.66 F1 versus free, exact structure ([arXiv 2508.19428](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19428v1)) |
| Seeded core ontology | Small human-owned schema (Product, Version, Feature, Procedure, Step, UIElement, Setting, ErrorCode, Role, BusinessObject, Term); LLM *types into* it, and new types go to review via EDC-style define-and-canonicalize | Typing ≫ induction; EDC canonicalization ([arXiv 2404.03868](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03868v1)) |
| Glossary / termbase | SKOS: prefLabel, altLabel, deprecated/hidden labels, quoted definitions with span provenance, scope notes per product and version; statistical term extraction unioned with LLM candidates to fix recall | Recall 0.30 for LLM-only ([arXiv 2508.19428](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19428v1)); termbase drift pattern ([Acrolinx](https://support.markup.ai/hc/en-us/articles/10228296536082)) |
| Operational KG | Property graph (e.g., Neo4j) with version-scoped assertions, deterministic IDs, scope-blocked entity resolution, and per-edge source span, model, prompt version and confidence; RDF/SHACL export for validation | ID minting matters most ([Edge Hill](https://research.edgehill.ac.uk/en/publications/deterministic-and-trustworthy-llm-driven-semantic-knowledge-graph/)); KGGen resolution ([arXiv 2502.09956](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09956v2)) |
| Issue detectors | Deterministic first (SHACL gaps, undefined terms, deprecated terms, version supersession), then rule-blocked contradiction candidates on typed facts with conditions, verified by LLM against spans; severity ≈ impact × reach × confidence; cluster issues by root entity | Formalize-then-reason ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10574434)); flag-rate cap ([arXiv 2511.00215](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00215v1)) |
| Q&A retrieval | Glossary normalization and entity linking → question classification → hybrid dense + BM25 always, plus graph traversal for comparison, dependency, version and cross-manual questions, plus structure expansion → merge, rerank, cap tokens → per-claim citations; abstention driven by chunk evidence; community summaries only for explicit global questions | Integration +6.4 vs selection +1.1; null-query collapse at 13.95% ([Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v2)) |
| Answer behaviour | Explicit Answer/Ask/Abstain policy; clarifying questions from ontology ambiguity (product, version, role); a "documentation notes" block from the issue store for intersecting entities | Default RAG rarely clarifies or abstains ([arXiv 2605.25831](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25831)); conflict prompting helps ([arXiv 2506.08500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08500v2)) |
| Reviewer agent | Queue and state in a database, not in LLM context; session state machine (dashboard → agreed goal → prioritized loop → summary); evidence spans checked by string match; agent cannot commit; separate critic pass blind to admin sentiment | IDEA2 loop and provenance ([arXiv 2604.01344](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01344v1)); sycophancy ([arXiv 2310.13548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)) |
| Review UX | Priority = impact × uncertainty × error-likelihood; batch by subtree or chapter; sessions well under 100 items; decide-before-reveal for high-impact items; show counter-evidence and alternatives; reason codes on rejection; second approver for merges, deletions and top-level restructuring; monitor rubber-stamp rates | Automation bias ([arXiv 2509.08514](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08514v1)); cognitive forcing ([arXiv 2102.09692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692v1)) |
| Governance and feedback | Versioned store (Git-backed SKOS/Turtle or named graph per release) with diffs and semver; PROV-style decision records; accepted decisions become SHACL and competency-question regression tests; rejections become negative few-shot examples; per-relation acceptance rates recalibrate auto-accept thresholds; resolved issues become preferred-source rules for Q&A | ODK PR + CI workflow ([OBO](https://obofoundry.org/COB/odk-workflows/EditorsWorkflow/)); ACTC thresholds ([arXiv 2305.06395](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.06395v3.pdf)) |

Two sequencing choices follow from the cost and evidence profile. First, build the glossary, structure graph, hybrid retrieval and deterministic detectors before any full GraphRAG community index. That stack delivers most of the expert-like behaviour at vector-RAG cost. Community summaries can come later, ideally lazily, if global "what does this product family do" questions turn out to matter. Second, build the evaluation harness before tuning anything. It needs a per-product gold set containing underspecified, unanswerable, conflicting-source and cross-manual questions, not just answerable single-hop ones. It also needs about 100 labelled flags per detector type to calibrate thresholds, and the admins' accept/reject rates as a live precision signal. No public benchmark exists for user manuals, so every gain claimed for this system has to be measured on its own corpus.

## Conclusion

The research changes the way the question should be posed. The knowledge graph is not mainly a better retriever; on typical manual questions it is barely one. It is a *verification substrate*. Formalizing manual content into scoped, versioned, provenance-linked facts is what turns contradiction detection from a ~31%-noise LLM guess into a rule-based candidate generator, what turns gap detection into constraint checks, and what lets the answering assistant attach known issues to exactly the entities a user asks about. The glossary is the cheapest artifact with the broadest reach, and the ontology's main value is scoping and disambiguation. The case for building them is strongest when they are governed, so the Reviewer assistant is less an optional extra than the component that makes the other two journeys trustworthy.

The open risk sits in the review loop itself, and the evidence points to a counterintuitive design stance. The more helpful and fluent the Reviewer agent appears, the more it will pull a busy admin toward rubber-stamping wrong proposals. Its success should therefore be measured by caught errors and by consistency that holds across releases, not by acceptance rate or speed. The deliberate friction that users dislike, such as decide-before-reveal, counter-evidence and second approvers for structural changes, should be reserved for the small set of high-impact decisions where it pays off. Because no one has yet measured a proactive, multi-session review agent end to end, building one with proper telemetry would itself be a novel contribution, not just an implementation exercise.
